This Cross-Border Data Transfer Addendum ("Addendum") supplements the BizMORI Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") and Privacy Policy. It sets forth the specific terms governing the international transfer of Personal Data in connection with the BizMORI service, in compliance with the Korea Personal Information Protection Act ("PIPA") and the EU General Data Protection Regulation ("GDPR").

---

## 1. Definitions

Terms used in this Addendum shall have the meanings defined in the DPA unless otherwise specified below.

- **"Data Exporter"** means the party transferring Personal Data to a recipient in another country.
- **"Data Importer"** means the party receiving Personal Data from a Data Exporter in another country.
- **"PIPA"** means the Korean Personal Information Protection Act (개인정보 보호법), as amended.
- **"PIPA Article 28-8"** means Article 28-8 of PIPA, governing the cross-border transfer of personal information.
- **"SCCs"** means the Standard Contractual Clauses approved by European Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914.
- **"UK Addendum"** means the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Commission Standard Contractual Clauses, issued by the UK ICO.

## 3. PIPA Article 28-8 Compliance (한국 개인정보의 국외이전)

### 3.1 Legal Basis for Transfer

Under PIPA Article 28-8, cross-border transfers of personal information are permitted where:
- The data subject has been informed and has given consent; or
- The transfer is necessary for the performance of a contract with the data subject or for the data subject's benefit; or
- The transfer is made pursuant to a contract that includes safeguards for the data subject's rights (위탁 계약).

MORI relies on the contractual safeguards basis under PIPA Article 28-8(1)(3) and Article 26 (위탁) for transfers from Korea, in accordance with the Personal Information Protection Commission's Guidelines on Cross-Border Transfer of Personal Information (개인정보의 국외 이전에 관한 고시).

**Distinction Between Delegation (위탁) and Third-Party Provision (제3자 제공):** Transfers to MORI SOLUTION, Inc. and AWS are categorized as delegation (위탁) under PIPA Article 26, as these entities process data on behalf of and under the instructions of MORI Corp. Transfers to Clerk, Sentry, and the payment processor are also categorized as delegation (위탁), as these sub-processors process data solely for the specific purposes delegated by MORI Corp. in connection with the Service.

### 3.3 Security Measures by Recipients

Each recipient of cross-border transfers shall maintain the following security measures:

| Measure | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Encryption in transit** | TLS 1.2+ on all connections |
| **Encryption at rest** | AES-256 or equivalent |
| **Access control** | Role-based access control; principle of least privilege |
| **Authentication** | Multi-factor authentication for administrative access |
| **Monitoring** | Continuous security monitoring and logging |
| **Incident response** | Documented incident response procedures; notification within 72 hours |
| **Contractual safeguards** | Written data processing agreement with each Sub-processor |

### 3.4 Contractual Safeguards

The Intercompany Service Agreement between MORI SOLUTION, Inc. and MORI Corp. includes the following safeguards for cross-border transfers:
- Processing only within the scope of the delegated purpose
- Technical and managerial security measures per PIPA Article 29
- Prohibition on sub-delegation without consent
- Indemnification for damages caused by violations
- Regular auditing and compliance verification

## 4. GDPR Compliance (EEA/UK Transfers)

### 4.1 Transfer Mechanisms

**Transfers to Korea:** South Korea has been granted an adequacy decision by the European Commission (Commission Implementing Decision of December 17, 2021, effective under GDPR Article 45). The UK has also recognized Korea's adequate level of data protection. Accordingly, transfers of Personal Data from the EEA to MORI Corp. in Korea do not require additional safeguards under GDPR Article 46.

**Transfers to the United States:** The SCCs apply to transfers from the EEA to the United States: (a) Module 2 (Controller to Processor) between Customer and MORI SOLUTION, Inc.; (b) Module 3 (Processor to Sub-processor) between MORI SOLUTION, Inc. and its U.S.-based sub-processors. The specific terms are set forth in Annex I of the DPA.

**Transfers from the UK:** The UK Addendum to the EU SCCs applies in addition to the SCCs for transfers from the United Kingdom, as detailed in Annex III of the DPA.

### 4.2 Transfer Impact Assessment

In accordance with the Schrems II decision (CJEU, Case C-311/18) and EDPB Recommendations 01/2020, MORI has assessed the level of data protection in the United States:

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Nature of data transferred** | API metadata, account information, images for processing. Images are processed and deleted per retention schedule; not stored long-term. |
| **Transfer mechanism** | SCCs Module 2 (Controller to Processor) |
| **Legal framework of destination** | U.S. law; EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (DPF) established by European Commission adequacy decision of July 10, 2023. Sub-processor DPF certification status: Clerk, Sentry, and Stripe are DPF certified. Current certification details are available at docs.bizmori.com/legal/sub-processors. MORI monitors DPF adequacy decision stability. |
| **U.S. surveillance laws** | FISA §702 and Executive Order 12333 may permit U.S. government access to data. Risk is mitigated by: (a) BizMORI processes B2B image data, which is unlikely to be a target of national security surveillance; (b) data is encrypted in transit and at rest; (c) MORI commits to challenging overbroad or unlawful access requests; (d) EO 14086 provides proportionality and necessity safeguards. |
| **Supplementary measures** | Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+) and at rest (AES-256); pseudonymization where possible; access controls; contractual commitments; minimal data retention |

### 4.3 Supplementary Measures

The following supplementary measures are implemented for transfers to the United States:
- End-to-end encryption of all data in transit using TLS 1.2+
- Encryption at rest using industry-standard encryption algorithms
- Minimization of Personal Data transferred (only what is necessary for the service)
- Short retention periods (processing files deleted immediately; logs retained for 90 days maximum)
- Contractual commitments from all Sub-processors to challenge disproportionate access requests
- Transparency reporting: MORI will disclose the existence and nature of government access requests (to the extent permitted by law) in an annual transparency summary

## 6. Ongoing Monitoring and Review

6.1. MORI shall monitor developments in data protection laws in all relevant jurisdictions and update this Addendum as necessary.

6.2. The transfer impact assessment (Section 4.2) shall be reviewed at least annually or upon any material change in the legal framework of the destination country.

6.3. If MORI determines that it can no longer ensure an adequate level of protection for transferred Personal Data, MORI shall promptly notify affected Customers and, if necessary, suspend or modify the relevant transfers.

6.4. **Encryption Key Location.** Encryption keys used for data protection are managed within secure key management infrastructure in South Korea. MORI does not transfer encryption keys to jurisdictions outside of Korea unless strictly necessary and subject to equivalent security measures.

6.5. **Fallback Mechanism.** In the event that a transfer mechanism (e.g., SCCs, adequacy decision, DPF) is invalidated by a court or regulatory authority, MORI shall: (a) promptly notify affected Customers; (b) within 30 days, implement an alternative lawful transfer mechanism; (c) if no alternative mechanism is available, minimize the data transferred and, if necessary, suspend transfers until a lawful mechanism can be established.

6.6. **Transparency Report.** MORI will publish an annual transparency summary by March 31 of each year, covering the prior calendar year. The summary will include: (a) the number and types of government data access requests received; (b) the jurisdictions from which requests originated; (c) the number of requests complied with; (d) any challenges to government requests. The summary will be published at docs.bizmori.com/legal/transparency.

6.7. **Data Subject Remedies.** Data subjects whose personal information has been transferred internationally and who believe their rights have been violated may: (a) contact MORI at mori@mori-corp.io; (b) lodge a complaint with the relevant supervisory authority (개인정보보호위원회 for Korea, relevant DPA for EU/UK); (c) seek remedies through the dispute resolution mechanisms in the DPA and Terms of Service.
