## 1. Definitions

- **"Applicable Data Protection Law"** means all applicable laws relating to data protection and privacy, including GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, Korea PIPA, and any implementing legislation.
- **"Controller"** means the entity that determines the purposes and means of Processing Personal Data (Customer).
- **"Data Subject"** means the identified or identifiable natural person to whom Personal Data relates.
- **"DPIA"** means a Data Protection Impact Assessment as described in GDPR Article 35.
- **"Personal Data"** means any information relating to a Data Subject that is processed by MORI in connection with the Service.
- **"Processing"** means any operation performed on Personal Data, including collection, storage, use, disclosure, or deletion.
- **"Processor"** means the entity that processes Personal Data on behalf of the Controller (MORI).
- **"SCCs"** means the Standard Contractual Clauses approved by the European Commission Decision 2021/914.
- **"Sub-processor"** means a third party engaged by MORI to process Personal Data on behalf of Customer.
- **"Security Incident"** means a confirmed unauthorized access, disclosure, or loss of Personal Data.
- **"UK Addendum"** means the International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU Commission Standard Contractual Clauses, issued by the UK Information Commissioner under Section 119A of the Data Protection Act 2018.

## 5. Security Measures

5.1. MORI shall implement and maintain appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect Personal Data, including:

**Encryption:**
- At rest: Industry-standard encryption for databases, file storage, and data stores
- In transit: TLS 1.2+ enforced on all connections
- API Keys: Industry-standard cryptographic hashing (no plaintext storage)
- Webhooks: Cryptographic signature verification

**Network Security:**
- Web Application Firewall (WAF) with managed rules
- Rate limiting
- Isolated network architecture for compute workloads
- Network flow monitoring

**Access Control:**
- Role-based access control (RBAC)
- Multi-layer authentication
- Secure administrative access (no direct SSH)
- Input validation and security headers

**Infrastructure:**
- Multi-region cloud infrastructure with redundancy
- Compute workloads in isolated private networks
- Automated database backups with retention
- Auto-scaling based on demand

**Monitoring:**
- Error and performance monitoring
- Infrastructure monitoring and alerting
- Automated health checks with failure detection

**Key Management:**
- Managed encryption keys with automatic key rotation
- Server-side encryption for all stored data
- Encryption keys are stored and managed within secure key management infrastructure in South Korea
- Access to key management operations is restricted to authorized personnel via access control policies

**Incident Response Plan:**
- MORI maintains a documented Incident Response Plan (IRP) that is tested at least annually
- The IRP includes identification, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review phases
- MORI conducts tabletop exercises and simulated breach scenarios to validate response readiness

## 8. Security Incidents

8.1. **Notification.** MORI shall notify Customer of a confirmed Security Incident without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware of the incident. MORI shall also comply with the breach notification requirements of all applicable U.S. state laws, which may require notification within shorter timeframes.

8.1A. **Government Access Requests.** If MORI receives a request from a government authority for access to Customer's Personal Data, MORI shall: (a) review the request for legal validity; (b) where legally permitted, promptly notify Customer before disclosing any data; (c) challenge requests that are overbroad or legally deficient; (d) disclose only the minimum amount of data required. MORI shall not provide government authorities with direct or systematic access to Personal Data.

8.2. **Content of Notification.** The notification shall include: (a) the nature of the incident; (b) categories and approximate number of Data Subjects affected; (c) likely consequences; (d) measures taken or proposed to address the incident; (e) contact details for MORI's incident response team.

8.3. **Cooperation.** MORI shall cooperate with Customer and take reasonable steps to mitigate the effects of the Security Incident, including providing timely updates and preserving evidence.

10.3. **Costs.** Audits shall be conducted at Customer's expense, including any reasonable costs incurred by MORI in facilitating the audit (such as personnel time and access arrangements). MORI shall provide a reasonable estimate of such costs before the audit commences.

10.4. **Alternative Assurance.** MORI may satisfy audit requests by providing relevant certifications, audit reports (e.g., SOC 2 Type II), penetration test summaries, or other documentation demonstrating compliance. MORI shall make SOC 2 Type II reports available to Customer upon request at least annually. If such documentation reasonably addresses Customer's audit objectives, Customer shall accept such documentation in lieu of an on-site audit.

10.5. **Scope.** Audits shall be limited to MORI's processing activities related to Customer's Personal Data and shall not extend to other customers' data or MORI's proprietary systems unrelated to the processing.
